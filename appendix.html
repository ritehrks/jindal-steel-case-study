1. The canonical model — locked figures and rulings

Full text: research/22-canonical-model-v1.md · every figure in the deck and calculator traces here

During pre-submission audit we found three conflicts across the research base and resolved each with a written ruling. The rulings, not the underlying files, govern all submitted numbers.

  • CBAM dataset. Two candidate datasets existed. We locked the traceable one: India stainless default 6.49 tCO₂e/t base rising to 8.44 with the +10/20/30% no-data markup (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621, corrected 2026/1740); ETS price path €80→€109 (2026–30); phase-in 2.5%→48.5% (Directive (EU) 2023/959). Liability quoted on the analyst-standard pro-rata basis.
  • Jindal's CBAM-relevant intensity. A single-plant Scope 1+2 figure (0.65) was rejected: CBAM counts company-wide direct emissions. Derived company-wide Scope 1 from BRSR absolutes: FY24 1.70 (matches CDP exactly), FY25 1.53, FY26 ≈ 1.52 tCO₂e/tcs. Because captive-power emissions count as indirect under CBAM Annex II, the realistic CBAM-direct basis is 0.9–1.1; we quote 1.4–1.5 as the conservative bound.
  • Benchmark comparisons. Jindal's S1+2 (1.76) must not be compared with Outokumpu's 1.8 (a cradle-to-gate figure including upstream Scope 3). Correct comparisons: cradle-to-gate 3.03 vs worldstainless global average ≈3.70; S1+2 1.76 vs Indian steel-sector ≈2.5.
  • Boundary rules. R1: captive ferrochrome and captive power live inside Scope 1+2 — never double-counted as Scope 3. R2: scrap enters burden-free (ISSF recycled-content); end-of-life credit is a second output. R3: intensities per tonne of crude steel (derived: FY24 1.758 Mt, FY25 1.956 Mt, FY26 2.015 Mt); sales tonnes are never the denominator.
  • Renewable share. 47% locked for FY26 full-year (assured ESG Factsheet basis); the "56% by Q3 FY26" figure is quoted only as dated momentum.

4. Methodology alignment: worldstainless / ISSF

File 06 · ISO 14040/14044, ISO 14404, GHG Protocol, worldsteel LCI
  • Standard boundary: cradle-to-gate (upstream alloys = Scope 3; melting/refining and downstream = Scope 1+2). Upstream Scope 3 is 60–75%+ of the footprint on virgin/NPI routes — the alloy mix moves total carbon far more than furnace efficiency.
  • Recycled-content method (cut-off): scrap enters burden-free; virgin alloys carry full upstream burden. Used for factory-gate reporting and CBAM. End-of-life method (IISI Appendix 5): X = X₁ + (X₂ − X₁)·RR·Y with RR ≈ 0.95, Y ≈ 0.909 — reported as our second output.
  • Benchmark curve (worldstainless): 85% scrap → 1.95 · 75% → 2.45 · 50% → 3.70 · 30% → 6.80 tCO₂/t. Rule of thumb: each +10pp scrap ≈ −0.8 to −0.9 tCO₂/t.

The verification dividend. €372–398 saved per tonne by 2030 by filing verified plant data instead of accepting EU defaults. On Jindal's 60–80 kt/yr EU export book: €22–32M ≈ ₹200–290 crore per year. The 2029→2030 phase-in jump (22.5%→48.5%) is the financial cliff that makes tooling urgent now.

Notes: for iron and steel the definitive phase prices direct emissions only (indirect/electricity excluded while EU producers receive indirect-cost compensation); post-consumer scrap carries zero embedded emissions, pre-consumer scrap carries its origin burden — mass-balance traceability is required to avoid punitive defaults.

7. EU market access beyond CBAM: CPR, ESPR, Digital Product Passport

Files 12, 20 · Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 (CPR), 2024/1781 (ESPR), CEN/CENELEC EN 18216–18223
  • CPR: from 8 Jan 2026, steel placed on the EU construction market must declare GWP (EN 15804+A2, product-specific, facility-specific) as a condition of CE marking. Generic industry averages no longer permitted.
  • ESPR / DPP: iron and steel are first-wave priority products; delegated act expected Q4 2026, mandatory compliance ≈2028. The Digital Product Passport is a decentralised, machine-readable record (heat number, chemistry, recycled content, GWP) using EN 18219 identifiers, QR data carriers, JSON-LD/REST interfaces.
  • Green procurement: the proposed Industrial Accelerator Act would require ≥25% low-carbon steel in EU public procurement from 2029 — no geographic restriction, an open opportunity for verified low-carbon exporters.
  • Implication: the calculator's per-tonne record is designed as the natural feeder for EPD/DoPC and DPP data fields — compliance infrastructure, not just an estimator.

8. Jindal's EU exposure: exports, quotas, competitive position

File 19 · CARE Ratings, JSL results, Iberjindal disclosures, UN Comtrade, Goldman Sachs / ICRA / CRISIL
  • Sales volume 2.17 → 2.56 Mt (FY24→FY26); export mix deliberately moderated 17% → 8%; estimated direct EU shipments 60–80 kt/yr, ~30 kt processed through Iberjindal S.L. (Lucena, Spain; ~€60M turnover).
  • Mid-2026 EU safeguard revision: tariff-free quota cut 47%; out-of-quota duty doubled to 50%; India-exclusive quota (Cat. 14 bars/sections) 92,557 t. CRISIL: 25–30% decline in Indian steel exports to EU; EEPC: ~$1.36B revenue at risk.
  • Analyst context: Goldman Sachs — Indian BF-BOF steel faces $102–190/t CBAM cost (15–28% margin compression); ICRA — $50–140/t through 2034. Jindal's scrap-EAF route with verified data is the structural exception — positioned to fill the supply vacuum as carbon-intensive routes exit the EU market.

10. The scrap ceiling: tramp elements and sensor sorting

File 11 + file 01 §E
  • Cu and Sn are noble to iron at 1600 °C — they cannot be oxidised out in the AOD and accumulate linearly with scrap ratio, causing surface hot-shortness (liquid Cu-rich films penetrating grain boundaries during hot rolling; Sn depresses the solidus below 900 °C and worsens it).
  • Supply side: India's scrap share is only 23–25% of steel input; 48 countries had scrap export restrictions (Mar 2025); the global scrap market is projected to swing to a ~15 Mt deficit by 2030. Hence the 80% hard cap in the tool.
  • The escape path is at the scrap yard, not the furnace: AI-driven XRF/XRT/LIBS sensor sorting (≈20 t/h lines) upgrades tramp-laden obsolete scrap; abatement cost ≈$30–43/tCO₂ — well below the 2030 CBAM price of €109.

11. Advanced levers (roadmap depth)

Files 09, 10, 13, 14, 15
  • Biochar in ferrochrome SAFs (Odisha): bamboo biochar (2–5% ash, 81–85% fixed C) is a near-drop-in for met coke; resistivity converges with fossil carbon above ~1400 °C; paddy-straw biochar capped ~10–20% by slag chemistry. 30% substitution abates 0.45–0.66 tCO₂/t FeCr; marginal abatement cost ≈$29–45/tCO₂. Aligned with the Ministry of Steel's 2024 "Greening the Steel Sector" roadmap.
  • Slag valorisation: AOD slag self-pulverises (β→γ dicalcium-silicate "dusting") — ideal for mineral carbonation without grinding; processed slags substitute cement clinker (Jindal–Harsco $150M contract expansion already on record). Dual benefit: Scope 1 mineralisation + Scope 3 avoided emissions.
  • Logistics: shifting inbound bulk from road (55–101 gCO₂e/t-km) to the electrified Dedicated Freight Corridor (11.5 g) saves ~40–67 kg CO₂e/tcs depending on route basis — scalable at near-zero marginal cost.
  • Internal carbon shadow pricing: a dynamic CBAM-aligned shadow price inside melt-shop charge optimisation (MILP) flips NPI-vs-scrap procurement decisions before the 2030 cliff arrives.
  • Captive power value stacking: replacing captive coal with hybrid renewables stacks LCOE savings + CCTS credits + CBAM avoidance + RCO compliance to ≈₹6.6/kWh of combined value — the transition pays for itself.

12. Assumptions, limitations and source register

  • Intensities per tonne of crude steel; Scope 2 market-based; CBAM module prices direct emissions only (conservative — captive-electricity emissions count as indirect under CBAM and would lower Jindal's basis further).
  • Model constants are calibrated so disclosed FY24/FY26 states reproduce audited intensities (±2%); ferromolybdenum ±30%; NPI 60–85 t/t range reflects sourcing variation; ETS path is analyst consensus, not a forecast guarantee.
  • The 80% scrap cap is a metallurgical-plus-supply judgment for critical grades; sensor sorting can raise it over time.
