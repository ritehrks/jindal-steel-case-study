## Executive Summary
The global stainless steel industry is navigating a profound structural realignment, driven by evolving geopolitical trade architectures, decarbonization mandates, and shifting demand profiles. For Indian manufacturers, the EU has historically served as a critical, high-margin export destination. However, safeguard quotas, anti-dumping duties (ADD), and the CBAM have fundamentally altered the economic calculus of exporting to the EU27.

## 3. India's Stainless Steel Trade with EU27

Total Indian finished steel exports fell from 7.5 MT (FY24) to 4.9 MT (FY25), a **34.7% decline**.

### Key Trade Dynamics:
- **HS 7219/7220 (Flat-rolled)**: Subject to EU anti-dumping duties under Regulation (EU) 2021/2012, specifically targeting India and Indonesia. Covers virtually the entire CR portfolio.
- **HS 7222 (Bars/Rods)**: Maintained stronger momentum (USD 543M). Different quota categories allow continued access.
- **HS 7223 (Wire)**: India holds **16.5% global market share** (USD 405M total). Key EU destinations: Netherlands ($53.4M), Germany ($29.7M), France ($26.6M).

## 4. EU Safeguard Quotas — Devastating Impact

Revised mid-2026 safeguard measures:
- **47% cut** in overall tariff-free steel import quota (capped at 18.3 MT annually)
- **Out-of-quota duty doubled**: 25% → **50%**
- India's exclusive quotas: Category 14 (stainless bars/sections) = 92,556.8 tonnes; Category 22 (seamless tubes) = 15,328.5 tonnes
- Category 15 (wire rod): **No exclusive India quota** — must compete in residual pool

### Analyst Impact Estimates:
| Source | Projected Export Decline |
|---|---|
| CRISIL Intelligence | 25-30% decline in Indian steel exports to EU |
| EEPC India | **40% reduction** = ~USD 1.36 billion annual revenue loss for India |

## 8. Strategic Implications

### The Domestic Pivot
- "Export is not a compulsion" — JSL management (FY25/FY26 earnings calls)
- Domestic consumption compounding at 8-10% annually (railways, aerospace, processing)
- EBITDA/tonne rose to INR 21,670 despite subdued global pricing

### Upstream Security
- 49% stake in Indonesian NPI smelter (New Yaking Pte Ltd)
- 1.2 MTPA steel melt shop via PT Glory in Indonesia
- Indonesian slabs → Jajpur for CR processing (capacity expanding to 2.67 MTPA by FY28)
- Controls end-to-end carbon footprint for cradle-to-gate CBAM reporting

### Long-Term EU Opportunity
As CBAM forces exit of carbon-intensive BF-BOF steel from EU → **supply vacuum**. JSL positioned to fill it with:
- 70% scrap utilization
- Green H2 deployment
- Hybrid renewable PPAs
- Verified 0.65 tCO2e/t emissions (vs default penalty of 3.167+ tCO2e/t)
- Localized processing via Iberjindal

**JSL's CBAM liability by 2030: €22/tonne vs competitors' €85-€107/tonne**
