# Scope 3 Logistics Decarbonization in the Indian Steel Sector: A Comparative Analysis of Heavy-Duty Trucking and Dedicated Freight Corridors

## Executive Summary
The Indian iron and steel industry operates at the nexus of rapid national infrastructure expansion and urgent global decarbonization mandates. As the second-largest producer of crude steel globally, the nation relies heavily on carbon-intensive blast furnace technologies and vast, geographically dispersed supply chains for the movement of heavy bulk raw materials such as iron ore, coking coal, and limestone. While the industry's primary decarbonization focus remains anchored on Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, Scope 3 emissions, particularly Category 4 (Upstream Transportation and Distribution), represent a highly actionable and scalable decarbonization lever.

This report provides an exhaustive techno-economic and environmental analysis of the GHG emission implications associated with shifting the transport of heavy bulk steel raw materials from traditional heavy-duty diesel trucks to the newly operational, fully electrified Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC) rail network in India. Utilizing the ISO 14083 standard and the Global Logistics Emissions Council (GLEC) Framework, the analysis quantifies the well-to-wheel (WTW) emission intensities of both modalities. The empirical data indicates that electrified DFC rail yields an emission intensity of approximately 11.5 gCO2e/t-km, a stark contrast to the 55.1 to 101 gCO2e/t-km footprint generated by heavy-duty diesel trucking.

Through rigorous quantitative scenario modeling, this report assesses the significance of this modal shift on a steel plant's overall lifecycle emissions. Although the relative percentage savings—approximately 1.7% to 2.6% of total lifecycle emissions—may appear marginal against a baseline of 2.55 tonnes of CO2 per tonne of crude steel (tCO2/tcs), the absolute emissions mitigation is profound at scale. For a standard 10 million tonne per annum (MTPA) integrated steel plant, the systematic shift to DFC rail can abate upwards of 668,000 tonnes of CO2e annually.

## The Logistics Burden of Steelmaking
Steel manufacturing is an inherently logistics-intensive endeavor. The production of a single tonne of crude steel via the traditional BF-BOF route requires the intake of approximately 2.5 to 3.0 tonnes of heavy bulk raw materials. Specifically, this comprises roughly 1.4 tonnes of iron ore, 0.8 tonnes of coking coal, and 0.3 tonnes of limestone.

India faces structural supply chain vulnerabilities due to its heavy dependence on imported coking coal. This coal lands at major eastern and western maritime gateways, such as Haldia, Paradip, and JNPT. From these port facilities, the coal must be transported inland to integrated steel plants typically located in the mineral-rich belts of Jharkhand, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh. The typical inland rail distance for imported coking coal from port to plant is approximately 400 km. More broadly, the overall average lead distance for all freight originating on the Indian Railways (IR) network stood at 613 km in the 2023-24 fiscal year.

## The Well-to-Wheel (WTW) Imperative
A foundational advancement introduced by ISO 14083, which aligns seamlessly with the Smart Freight Centre’s GLEC Framework v3.01, is the strict mandate to account for emissions on a Well-to-Wheel (WTW) basis. The WTW metric provides a comprehensive view of the energy life cycle by amalgamating two distinct operational phases:
- **Well-to-Tank (WTT)**: Upstream emissions associated with extraction, refining, and distribution of fuel/electricity.
- **Tank-to-Wheel (TTW)**: Direct combustion emissions or consumption of electricity during traction.

## Road Freight: The Carbon Burden of Heavy-Duty Trucking
Indian heavy-duty trucks typically consume more fuel per tonne-kilometer (t-km) when compared to global averages. The SFC India Default GHG Emission Values establish:

| Vehicle Classification | WTT Intensity | TTW Intensity | Total WTW Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy Commercial Vehicle (Payload 20–40 MT) | 15.4 gCO2e/t-km | 50.9 gCO2e/t-km | 66.3 gCO2e/t-km |
| Tractor Trailer (Payload 20–50 MT) | 12.8 gCO2e/t-km | 42.3 gCO2e/t-km | 55.1 gCO2e/t-km |

## The Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC): A Paradigm Shift in Rail Logistics
The DFC project represents exclusive, high-speed, heavy-haul freight railways completely divorced from passenger traffic. The technological hallmark of the DFC network is its complete electrification. Traction is provided by advanced WAG-12B electric locomotives (12,000-horsepower) capable of hauling 6,000-tonne rakes. Crucially, the WAG-12B utilizes regenerative braking technology, which feeds electricity back into the overhead catenary system.

Specific operational data related directly to the modernized DFC network places the precise operational emission intensity of the electrified corridors at **11.5 gCO2/t-km**.

## Quantitative Scenario Modeling: The Logistics of a Tonne of Steel
**Scenario Parameters**
- Finished Product Unit: 1 Tonne of Crude Steel (1 tcs).
- Total Inbound Mass (M): 2.5 metric tonnes of raw materials.
- Distance B (Long Haul): 613 km.

**Calculation (613 km Haul)**
Total Transport Work = 2.5 tonnes × 613 km = 1,532.5 t-km.
- Trucking Emissions: 1,532.5 t-km × 55.1 gCO2e/t-km = 84.44 kg CO2e / tcs
- DFC Rail Emissions: 1,532.5 t-km × 11.5 gCO2e/t-km = 17.62 kg CO2e / tcs
- **Net Emissions Abatement: 66.82 kg CO2e / tcs**

## The Economic and Trade Perspective: EU CBAM
At a CBAM carbon price of €75.36/tonne, mitigating 66.8 kg of CO2e translates to a voided carbon liability of **€5.03 per tonne** of exported steel. In a highly commoditized global market where net margins are notoriously thin, securing a €5 per tonne cost advantage simply by optimizing the inbound logistics modality can determine market retention in premium European and North American zones.

## Strategic Conclusions
The systematic optimization of Scope 3 upstream logistics via the Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC) is a highly pragmatic, economically viable decarbonization strategy for the Indian steel industry. By migrating transport to the electrified DFC network, steel producers can realize verified emissions savings of between 43 and 80 kg of CO2e per tonne of crude steel produced. Unlike direct Scope 1 decarbonization technologies, shifting freight to the DFC leverages existing, fully operational infrastructure, delivering scalable carbon abatement at zero or negative marginal cost to the producer.
