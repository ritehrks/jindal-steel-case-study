# CBAM Financial Impact: Projected Tax Exposure for Indian Stainless Steel (2026–2030)

## Executive Summary

The European Union's **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** transitioned into its definitive, payment-linked phase on **January 1, 2026** (under Regulation (EU) 2023/956). From 2026 through 2030, EU importers of Indian stainless steel must purchase and surrender CBAM certificates reflecting the embedded carbon emissions of their shipments.

This report establishes three critical parameters:
1. **Expected EU ETS Carbon Price (2026–2030)**: Projected to trade in an annual average range of **€80.00/tCO2 in 2026** rising to **€109.00/tCO2 by 2030** (weighted 5-year average of **~€94.70/tCO2**).
2. **EU Default Emission Factors for Stainless Steel (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621, as corrected by (EU) 2026/1740)**:
   - For **India specifically**, the base default emission factor for stainless steel flat products (CN 7219 / 7220) and semi-finished ingots (CN 7218) is **6.49 tCO2e/t** (direct emissions only).
   - Under the EU’s mandatory punitive regulatory mark-up schedule (+10% in 2026, +20% in 2027, +30% in 2028–2030), the assigned default rises to **7.14 tCO2e/t in 2026**, **7.79 tCO2e/t in 2027**, and **8.44 tCO2e/t from 2028 to 2030**.
   - If country-specific data is absent, the fallback "Other countries and territories" global default is **4.94 tCO2e/t base** (rising to **5.43–6.42 tCO2e/t** with mark-ups).
3. **Projected CBAM Tax Exposure for Indian Exports (€/tonne)**:
   - **Under Punitive EU Defaults**: CBAM liability spans **€550 to €905 per tonne** under the direct regulatory formula (or **€14 to €446/t** under phased pro-rata gross scaling). This equals **20% to 32% of total export value**, completely eliminating commercial viability in Europe.
   - **Under Actual Verified Producer Data (Jindal Stainless)**: Leveraging an EAF + AOD route with >70% recycled scrap, Jindal's actual Scope 1 direct emissions (~**1.38 tCO2e/t** in FY26, dropping to **~1.00 tCO2e/t** by 2030) slashes the CBAM exposure to **€89–€94/t** under the direct regulatory formula (or **€2.20 to €53/t** under pro-rata scaling).
   - **Value Saved by Filing Actual Data**: Disclosing verified plant-level emissions saves **€460 to €810 per tonne** of stainless steel exported to the EU.

## 2. EU Default Emission Factors for Imported Stainless Steel

When an importer cannot provide independently verified, installation-specific emissions data from the manufacturer, the European Commission assigns mandatory default values.

### 2.1 Legal Framework: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621 & Correction (EU) 2026/1740
Adopted on December 16, 2025, and corrected on July 31, 2026, **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621** (amended by **(EU) 2026/1740**) establishes country-specific and global default values for all CBAM goods.

### 2.2 Scope of Emissions: Direct Emissions Only
Under **Annex II of Regulation (EU) 2023/956**, for iron and steel products (CN Chapters 72 and 73, except sintered iron ore CN 2601 12 00):
- **Only Direct Emissions (Scope 1)** are included in the calculation of embedded emissions and CBAM financial obligations.
- **Indirect Emissions (Scope 2 from purchased electricity) are EXCLUDED** during this stage because EU domestic steelmakers continue to receive indirect cost compensation (state aid) under EU ETS rules. In the official EU default value tables, the "Indirect emissions" column for stainless steel is marked as `—` (dash / zero liability).

## 3. CBAM Phase-In Schedule & Free Allocation Adjustment (FAA)

CBAM does not tax all embedded emissions at 100% immediately. Under **Directive (EU) 2023/959** and **CBAM Article 31**, CBAM liability phases in at the exact pace that free EU ETS allowances are phased out for EU domestic producers.

### 3.1 Phase-In Trajectory (2026–2030)

| **2027** | **5.0%** | **95.0%** |
| **2028** | **10.0%** | **90.0%** |
| **2029** | **22.5%** | **77.5%** |
| **2030** | **48.5%** | **51.5%** |
| *2031* | *61.0%* | *39.0%* |
| *2032* | *73.5%* | *26.5%* |
| *2033* | *86.0%* | *14.0%* |
| *2034* | *100.0% (Full Phase-in)* | *0.0% (Zero Free Allowances)* |

### 3.2 EU ETS / CBAM Benchmark for Stainless Steel
Under **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620**, the free allocation deduction is anchored to official EU ETS product benchmarks representing the average GHG performance of the top 10% most efficient EU installations:
- **Benchmark for EAF High-Alloy / Stainless Crude Steel ($\text{BM}$)**: **0.268 tCO2e per tonne** (Directive 2003/87/EC Phase 4 benchmark, with post-2026 proposed updates ranging between 0.176 and 0.268 tCO2e/t).
- **Specific Embedded Free Allocation ($\text{SEFA}$)** embedded in 1 tonne of imported steel:
  $$\text{SEFA}_y = \text{BM} \times (1 - \text{CBAM}_y)$$
  - 2026: $0.268 \times 97.5\% = \mathbf{0.261\text{ tCO2e/t}}$
  - 2027: $0.268 \times 95.0\% = \mathbf{0.255\text{ tCO2e/t}}$
  - 2028: $0.268 \times 90.0\% = \mathbf{0.241\text{ tCO2e/t}}$
  - 2029: $0.268 \times 77.5\% = \mathbf{0.208\text{ tCO2e/t}}$
  - 2030: $0.268 \times 51.5\% = \mathbf{0.138\text{ tCO2e/t}}$

## 4. Projected CBAM Tax Exposure Calculations (€/tonne)

Depending on how regulatory authorities and commercial counterparties apply the phase-in mechanics, two primary calculation models are used in industry financial projections:

- **Model 1: Definitive EU Regulatory Net Formula (Regulation (EU) 2025/2620)**
  The statutory EU legal rule ensuring non-discrimination under WTO GATT Article III:2: Importers surrender certificates for all embedded emissions exceeding the free allocation granted to EU producers.
  $$\text{Exposure (€/tonne)} = [E_{\text{embedded}} - \text{SEFA}_y] \times P_{\text{ETS}}$$
- **Model 2: Pro-Rata Phase-In Scaling Model (Trade Advisory & Market Analyst Model)**
  Widely utilized by trade bodies (e.g. GTRI, Wood Mackenzie, BigMint) where the annual CBAM factor (2.5% to 48.5%) scales the total liability on surplus emissions above the EU benchmark:
  $$\text{Exposure (€/tonne)} = [E_{\text{embedded}} - \text{BM}] \times \text{CBAM}_y \times P_{\text{ETS}}$$
  *(Or Gross Scaling: $E_{\text{embedded}} \times \text{CBAM}_y \times P_{\text{ETS}}$)*

### The Macroeconomic and Regulatory Genesis of the Definitive CBAM Regime
The fundamental architecture of the CBAM is designed to neutralize the risk of "carbon leakage". By imposing a border levy exactly equivalent to the domestic carbon price, the European Union aims to restore a level playing field while simultaneously applying coercive economic pressure on foreign jurisdictions to adopt reciprocal carbon pricing mechanisms. Beginning January 1, 2026, the mechanism enters its definitive phase, transforming the CBAM from an administrative reporting exercise into a hard, inescapable financial liability.

### Delineating the Stainless Steel Scope and Process Routes
For a competitive, modern Indian stainless steel manufacturer utilizing an optimized EAF route, the actual verified direct embedded emissions typically average around 0.90 tCO2e/t. It is critical to note that while EAF production consumes massive amounts of electrical power, generating substantial Scope 2 indirect emissions, the definitive phase of the CBAM primarily targets direct emissions and specific precursor emissions for the steel sector in its initial years.

### The Mathematical Architecture of CBAM Liability
The exact mathematical formulation for the number of CBAM certificates required is defined as follows:
`Cdue = Q × max(0, Ed − (FCBAM × CSCF × BEU))`
- `FCBAM`: CBAM phase-in factor.
- `CSCF`: Cross-Sectoral Correction Factor (1.000 for 2026-2030).
- `BEU`: Specific EU ETS product benchmark (0.176 tCO2e/t for high-alloy steel).

### Article 9 Offsets and the Indian Domestic Carbon Pricing Argument
Importers may claim a reduction under Article 9 if a carbon price has already been paid in the country of origin. India's composite argument rests on:
1. **GST Compensation Cess on Coal**: Rs 400 per tonne.
2. **PAT Scheme**: Energy Saving Certificates.
3. **Renewable Purchase Obligations (RPO)**.

Isolating the GST Cess (Rs 400/t coal), it translates to roughly €2.00 per tonne of CO2. While this is a marginal financial victory (reducing an €85/t liability to €83/t), the European Commission's acceptance of the PAT scheme as an "explicit" carbon price remains a significant regulatory unknown. Leading Indian corporations disclose these strictly as contingent assets.
